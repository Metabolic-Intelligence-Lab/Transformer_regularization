# Protocol P1 — results summary (XS matrix + S confirmation)

Status: all pre-registered runs complete (2026-11-14). Tables and statistics are generated by
`analysis/p1_analysis.py` into `results/p1_report.md`; this file is the narrative reading of them.
Protocol: `protocols/P1_minibench.md`. Raw results: `results/dc/*.json` (one file per method and arm,
with the tuning grid, the winner, and the final runs on seeds 1001–1003).

## Setup in one paragraph

Decoder-only GPT, two sizes (XS = 30.4M, S = 51.5M parameters), pinned 160M-token corpus, 2,432
optimizer steps for every run. Two data regimes with identical compute: **R16** (10M unique tokens
repeated for 16 epochs) and **R1** (159.4M unique tokens, one epoch). Thirteen methods, each tuned
on a learning-rate × method-hyperparameter grid with a single tuning seed (42), one grid extension
allowed per axis when the winner lands on an edge, then 3 final seeds (2 in R1) under the winner.
Primary metric: test loss at the best-validation checkpoint. Contrasts are paired by seed against
AdamW: paired t-test, Holm correction within each table, 10,000-resample percentile bootstrap CI,
and a paired TOST for parity with a ±0.01-nat margin. The S phase re-runs AdamW plus the four
best XS-R16 methods (AdamW+EMA, Muon, AdamS, Muon+Const; chosen mechanically).

## Headline findings

1. **AdamW with strong decoupled decay is the method to beat, and nothing beats it with
   significance.** In the primary table (XS, R16, n = 3) no contrast survives Holm. AdamW's tuned
   configuration is lr 4.5e-3, wd 2.5 in both XS and S.
2. **AdamW+EMA is the only method that never loses to AdamW, and its gain is small.** −0.024 nats
   vs AdamW in XS-R16 (CI95 [−0.084, +0.020]) and again −0.024 in S-R16 (CI95 [−0.033, −0.011],
   p = 0.069, Holm 0.21) with the three registered seeds. With six seeds at S (post-registration,
   below) the gain shrinks to −0.011 (CI95 [−0.025, +0.003], p = 0.21): not separated from zero.
   Cost: 0.1 % wall-clock.
3. **The constant-decay family is clearly worse under repetition.** Const +0.17, Const-Huber +0.13,
   Const-Fair +0.27, CPR +0.23, Baseline (no decay) +0.33, SF-AdamW +0.41 nats in XS-R16; all
   bootstrap intervals exclude zero, none survives Holm at n = 3. This includes the τ(w)-style
   Const-Fair variant: the paper's original claim does not hold in this protocol.
4. **Muon's advantage is a one-epoch phenomenon.** In R1 Muon beats AdamW by −0.238 nats (CI95
   [−0.263, −0.214]); in R16 the gap is −0.021 and not distinguishable from zero; at S Muon is
   *worse* by +0.051 (CI95 [+0.046, +0.055], Holm 0.010, pre-registered verdict "worse"). Muon
   and Muon+Const reach their best validation around epoch 10 and then overfit (final-checkpoint
   test 4.89 and 5.13 at S vs 4.44 and 4.39 at the best checkpoint): without early stopping they
   lose badly.
5. **Repetition changes the ranking.** Regime effect = (method − AdamW in R16) − (method − AdamW in
   R1). Baseline +0.34 [+0.27, +0.40], CPR +0.27, Muon +0.22 and CWD +0.07 lose ground when data are
   repeated; AdamW+EMA −0.15, AdamW+dropout −0.14, AdamS −0.07 and SF-AdamW −0.18 gain ground.
   The constant-decay methods have no regime interaction (≈ 0): they are worse in both regimes.
6. **In R1 weight decay buys nothing.** Baseline is within 0.012 nats of AdamW with 2 seeds
   (CI95 [−0.039, +0.017]).

## Independent replicate of the S phase

The S phase was run twice end to end, on two hosts (RTX A4500, 2026-11-10..12, and RTX 3090,
2026-11-12..14), same code, same seeds. The 3090 run is the one reported above; the A4500 run is
kept under `results/dc_replicate_S_A4500/`.

| Method | A4500 | 3090 | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| AdamW | 4.383 | 4.385 | same winner |
| Muon | 4.439 | 4.436 | same winner |
| Muon+Const | 4.393 | 4.391 | same winner |
| AdamW+EMA | 4.390 | 4.362 | winner flipped: ema 0.999 → 0.9999 |
| AdamS | 4.573 | 4.454 | winner flipped: lr 1.5e-3 → 1.35e-2 (via the edge extension) |

Where the tuning winner was the same, the two hosts agree within 0.003 nats. Where it differed,
the cause is GPU non-determinism acting on a single tuning seed: the same AdamS configuration
(lr 4.5e-3, wd 1e-5, seed 42) reached validation 4.87 on the A4500 and diverged to 6.22 on the
3090; its neighbour (wd 1e-4) scored 4.574 vs 4.551, which was enough to move the argmax, trigger
the edge extension, and change AdamS's final result by 0.12 nats. For EMA the two candidate
winners differ by 0.02–0.04 on the tuning seed in opposite directions on the two hosts.

## Limitations to state in the paper

- **Power.** n = 3 seeds per cell (2 in R1). The sd of the paired difference from AdamW is ~0.05
  nats at XS and ~0.01 at S, so a paired t-test at 80 % power detects ~0.16 nats at XS and ~0.03 at S
  (n = 6: 0.07 and 0.012). The Holm criterion is unreachable at XS; the bootstrap intervals, not the
  p-values, carry the information.
- **Single-seed tuning + edge rule is fragile** for methods near an instability boundary (AdamS,
  high-lr Muon, EMA at 0.999+). The replicate shows winner flips worth 0.03–0.12 nats. The ranking
  of the top group (EMA, AdamW, Muon+Const within 0.03 nats) should be read with this in mind.
- **Winners on grid edges after the one allowed extension:** AdamW itself is on the wd edge (2.5)
  in XS-R16 and S, so a stronger decay could improve the standard further; this can only
  strengthen the main conclusion, but AdamW is not provably at its optimum. Const-family winners
  sit on the rho edge; Muon on the wd edge.
- **SF-AdamW is slow at this horizon, not broken** (worst method in both regimes, +0.41 and
  +0.59 nats). Code audit (2026-11-14): reference package, warm-up passed, train/eval switching
  around every evaluation, decoupled decay with AdamW's per-step rate. Both train and validation loss
  lag AdamW throughout; the evaluated iterate is a uniform average of all post-warm-up iterates and
  2,432 steps are too few. The wider grid (below) finds no better point.
- **Early stopping is part of the protocol.** Methods that overfit after epoch 10 (Muon,
  Muon+Const, Baseline, CPR) are credited with their best checkpoint; at the final checkpoint they
  are far worse (see the end-of-training table in `p1_report.md`).
- **Scale.** 30M and 51M parameters, 10M unique tokens in the repeated arm. Conclusions are about
  the heavily over-parameterised, data-constrained corner, not about frontier pretraining.

## Post-registration sensitivity runs (2026-11-14, one community RTX 3090, ~6 GPU-hours)

Tagged result files (`_x6`, `_wdx`, `_audit`), each seeded from the registered journal; reported
under "Sensitivity analyses" in `p1_report.md` and in Section 5.2 / Appendix B of the paper. They
never replace the pre-registered files.

- **Six final seeds at S (`x6`)**, seeds 1004–1006 added for AdamW and AdamW+EMA. AdamW 4.3845
  (sd 0.0114), EMA 4.3734 (sd 0.0153); paired diff −0.0111, CI95 [−0.0245, +0.0027], p = 0.206,
  TOST(0.01) fails. Per-seed diffs: −0.027, −0.033, −0.011 (registered) and +0.012, +0.011, −0.018
  (added). The paired sd for this pair is 0.019 at n = 6 (0.011 at n = 3): six seeds detect 0.027
  nats for it, not the 0.012 predicted from three.
- **Second wd extension for AdamW at S (`wdx`)**, wd 7.5 at lr 5e-4, 1.5e-3, 4.5e-3: tuning val
  5.274 / 4.765 / 4.935 against 4.873 / 4.521 / 4.391 at wd 2.5. Winner unchanged (lr 4.5e-3,
  wd 2.5), finals are the registered ones. AdamW's wd optimum at S is interior (0.5 < wd* < 7.5).
  Not run: wd 7.5 at the lr extension 1.35e-2.
- **SF-AdamW wider grid at XS (`audit`)**, lr 4.5e-3, 1.35e-2, 4e-2, 0.12 × wd 2.5, 7.5, no finals:
  val 4.919 (registered winner), 5.257, 6.026, 6.763 at wd 2.5; 5.622, 5.888, 6.645, 7.389 at
  wd 7.5. Combined with the registered grid (lr 5e-4..1.35e-2, wd 0.1..2.5) the optimum is interior
  on both axes. The gap to AdamW is not under-tuning.

## Compute

XS matrix: 13 methods × 2 arms, tuning + finals, one RTX A4500 (2026-10-19..22). S phase:
5 methods, ~35 GPU-hours on one RTX 3090 (0.5 $/h, secure cloud) plus the A4500 replicate.
Sensitivity runs: 17 runs, ~6 GPU-hours on one community RTX 3090 (0.22 $/h, 2026-11-14).
Package 2 and P2: five to six community RTX 3090 pods each, 2026-11-15/16. All runs together:
567 distinct runs, about 170 GPU-hours of training time.
Throughput 125–128k tokens/s at S, 166–171k at XS; method overheads ≤ 2.4 % (Muon family).
